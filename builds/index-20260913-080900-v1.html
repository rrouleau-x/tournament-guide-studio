Questions

The things clubs ask first.

Do we need a new app for parents to install?

No. It's a web link, not an app store download. Parents open it in Safari and tap "Add to Home Screen" — it behaves like an app, works offline, and needs zero setup. Nothing to install from the App Store, nothing to approve.

How is this different from TeamSnap or our scheduling platform?

Those tools tell you when and where. They don't tell a parent which hotel is actually 12 minutes from the field, what time to leave given a 45-minute report time, whether the pop-up tent policy allows stakes, what's still pending from the tournament, or what to pack for a 105° heat index. That's the gap this fills. It sits alongside your existing tools rather than replacing them.

How current is the information?

Every fact carries a status label — Official Tournament Information, Confirmed Team Information, Parent Manager Recommendation, or Pending Confirmation. Prices, availability, forecasts, and drive estimates carry an "information current as of" date. We never turn an estimate into a requirement or a recommendation into a coach instruction. When something changes, the live link updates in place.

Can it carry our sponsors?

Yes, and this is the strongest reason to do it. Sponsor logos, a local business directory, and a single "presented by" slot are all built in. You keep the sponsorship revenue.

What about stay-to-play housing rules?

Handled carefully and separately. Official partner hotels are listed with the requirement explained plainly, and non-official alternatives are clearly marked as not-to-be-booked until a team manager confirms a proximity exception. Rule requirements are never presented as suggestions, and suggestions are never presented as rules.

Who writes it?

It's built by a parent manager who got tired of writing the same email forty times — and who has produced guides for real tournaments, including a live app used by families at a 91-team event. The whole approach comes from being the person in the group chat, not from being a software company.
